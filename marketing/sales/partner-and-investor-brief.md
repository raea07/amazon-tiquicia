# Partner and investor brief

**What it is.** A one-page brief and a ten-slide outline for two readers: a partner (a chamber of commerce, a producers' network, a bank) and an investor. Only facts from the repository and sourced market figures; the gaps are said plainly. Spanish and English side by side for the partner; English with Spanish headings for the investor.
**Conclusion 1.** What can be shown is four built and tested web apps and a sourced reason to exist (SINPE Móvil everywhere; a well-known cross-border store that takes international cards only and takes up to two weeks). What cannot be shown is any real sale, or an app running on a real phone.
**Conclusion 2.** The partner ask is small and free: a session with members and five pilot shops. The investor ask is not set; this file gives the facts, not the amount.
**Conclusion 3.** The three numbers that decide the model (margin per order, deliveries per driver-day, 60-day repeat) are assumptions until the pilot measures them. Every slide says so.
**The owner must decide** the three questions at the end (which partner first, whether to meet investors before the pilot, and the ask).

**Gates:** G1 and G2 before sending either brief. Re-read every market figure on its source the week the brief is used; most are press reports from 2025–2026.

| **Yuntera — qué es / what it is**                    | An online marketplace for Costa Rica with its own warehouse and drivers. Local shops sell; Yuntera stores, collects payment (SINPE Móvil, cash on delivery, cards) and delivers, starting in a cluster of cantons in the central GAM.                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **El problema / the problem**                        | Shoppers who pay by SINPE Móvil and want a parcel the next delivery day have few options we found: Amazon Bazaar accepts international cards only and takes up to two weeks (El Financiero, 9 Nov 2025; terms may have changed); Walmart's new marketplace takes cards and leaves delivery to sellers (Delfino, Jan 2026, sponsored). | **Cómo gana / how it earns**                         | A commission of 8%–15% on each sale (inside the 8%–15% Amazon publishes for general goods), a warehouse fee per unit, a payment fee set at about the gateway's published price (no contract yet), and the shopper's delivery charge (₡1,500 in the core zone, free from ₡25,000). All amounts are pilot assumptions.                                                                                                                                |

| **Lo que falta / what is missing**                   | No real sale. No app tested on a real phone. No legal entity, gateway contract or tax registration yet. No trademark search. No seller signed. Paid marketing waits for the pilot's results.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Lo que decide el modelo / what decides the model** | Three numbers, measured in a closed pilot of about 100 orders: margin per order (planning estimate ₡2,334 on a ₡20,000 order, ₡394 on ₡8,000), delivered stops per driver-day (at an assumed ₡45,000 driver-day, an own driver beats Correos' 451-or-more tier at about 24–26 stops before IVA, and at about 22 if the IVA on Correos cannot be credited; for the accountant to confirm), and the share of shoppers who order again within 60 days. |
| **El mes / the month**                               | [From the pricing proposal §3.3 and the go-to-market budget §9, once they agree: the result at three volumes, the break-even in orders a day, and the budget level chosen (go-to-market decision 4). Until both documents use the same staffing, leave this row out.]                                                                                                                                                                               |

The stops figure: replacing one driver at 23–25 stops × 26 days is about 600–650 parcels a month, which is Correos' 451-or-more tier (₡1,756.19 before IVA). ₡45,000 ÷ ₡1,756.19 = 25.6 stops; with the ₡2,000 daily pickup, (₡45,000 − ₡2,000) ÷ ₡1,756.19 = 24.5. If the IVA cannot be credited, ₡1,756.19 × 1.13 = ₡1,984.50 gives 22.7 and 21.7. Before Correos authorises the volume tariff, the ₡2,300 locker price applies (19.6 stops). The ₡45,000 driver-day is an assumption; legal-minimum labour is about ₡21,700 of it (market research §6.2, estimate). The other strategy documents must use the same figure; today they do not all agree.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **Which partner first?**
   - A. Correos' Dirección de Pymes, asking also for authorisation for the Pymexpress volume tariff (all four tiers need it), the pickup price, and a session for its 20,400 Pymexpress clients (2024 figure). **(recommended)** One meeting serves the courier fallback and seller recruitment.
   - B. MEIC (EXPOPYME and its registers).
   - C. A chamber of commerce in the pilot cantons.
2. **Meet investors before the pilot?**
   - A. No; send the brief only after 100 pilot orders. **(recommended)** Without a real sale, the three deciding numbers are guesses, and the brief says so.
   - B. Yes, to fund the pilot itself.
3. **What is the ask on slide 10?**
   - A. Leave it out until decision 2 is answered. **(recommended)** No amount has been worked out in the repository.
   - B. The owner writes an amount and use of funds now, from the go-to-market draft's budget levels.

# Roll-out plan

**What this is.** The phases from today's state to an open launch in the GAM core, each with its entry condition, work, owner, exit test and what is switched on in the product.
**Conclusion 1.** The software is not what gates the pilot; the company, the lawyer, the accountant, the ONVO contract and the Hacienda registration are, and only the owner can start them.
**Conclusion 2.** A closed pilot with real money and real parcels in 3 to 5 cantons comes before any public launch, and ends only on the roadmap's evidence (100 orders to standard), not on a date.
**Conclusion 3.** A canton is added only when the cantons already served fill their routes and keep their promises; the rule is in §3.
**Conclusion 4.** At best the pilot starts in week 10 and the open launch in week 23; if invoicing certification takes its full 8 weeks, the pilot starts in week 16 or later (estimates).
**The owner must decide** six questions, asked once in the [owner decisions](owner-decisions.md) list: when week 1 starts (D4), warehouse size (D11), pilot payment methods (D13), the cash ceiling (D14), the shopper app (D15) and pilot size (D16).

Weeks are counted from **week 1**, the start of phase 0 (owner decision D4). Week
numbers after phase 0 are **estimates**: no source gives the lead time of a
Costa Rican incorporation, a Hacienda registration or a gateway contract.
Each phase starts when its entry condition is true, whatever the week.

Related: [roadmap](../01-product/roadmap.md) (the phases of the business; this
plan details its phases 0 to 2), [go-live checklist](../07-deployment/go-live-checklist.md)
(not copied here), [go-to-market](go-to-market.md),
[team structure](team-structure.md), `STATUS.md` ("What needs you").

**Who:** O = owner · L = lawyer · A = accountant · B = the builders (the
people changing the code) · S = a supplier (ONVO, the messaging
provider, Correos, INS).

## 3. Zones: which cantons, and the rule for adding one

**First.** The pilot cluster of owner decision D10:
3 to 5 adjacent cantons of the 16 core cantons
(`supabase/migrations/20261121080300_geography_data.sql`). All other
districts are marked not served in **Operación → Distritos**.

**Add one canton when all five are true** (read weekly from the console and
the numbers sheet):

1. The served cantons averaged **at least 20 delivered stops per driver-day**
   over the last 2 weeks, or an existing route passes the new canton with room
   for **at least 5 more stops**. The test is 20, not the 25 at which an own
   driver beats Correos ([go-to-market](go-to-market.md) §6), on purpose: the
   added canton's stops are what lift a route from 20 towards 25. At 20 stops
   each order still contributes (orders lose money only below about 11).
2. On-time delivery **≥ 95%** and first-attempt success **≥ 90%** over the
   same 2 weeks.
3. The new canton **touches** a served canton.
4. A driver (or the backup) is available for the added stops on every
   delivery day.
5. At least one active seller or a pending order demand is known there. The
   product does not record refused checkouts outside the zone; until it does,
   count requests received by WhatsApp (a product gap, not built).

**Order.** Adjacent core cantons first, until all 16 are served; then GAM
extended cantons (the product's defaults there: order by 12:00, 1–2 delivery
days, ₡2,000, free from ₡35,000; all assumptions in
[pricing and fees](../01-product/pricing-and-fees.md)). The national zone
stays off until a carrier partnership exists (roadmap phase 4).

**Take one out** for new orders, with a zone closure in **Operación →
Cierres**, when its parcels are late more than 5% for 2 weeks running.
Shoppers already promised a date keep it.

**Who.** The owner (or the operations lead later) decides; staff with the
`ops` role change it in the console; every change is in **Auditoría**.

01

The idea

A yunta is a pair of oxen joined by a yoke so they pull as one. In Costa Rica it is what pulled the carreta, the cart that carried the country's coffee to port. To do something en yunta is to pull together.

Yuntera is a coined word built on that root. A marketplace is the same arrangement: two parties who each need the other, joined so the load moves. Say it yoon-TEH-rah. In running text it takes a capital Y; in the wordmark it is always lowercase.

One mark, three readings

  • The letter YThe initial of the name.
  • The yokeThe piece that joins two so they pull as one. The two nodes are the pair.
  • Two routes, one doorSeller and buyer, shop and driver, converging on a single destination.

02

Personality

Yuntera is the reliable local business that happens to run on software. The test for any new screen, message or advert: would the owner of a well-run neighbourhood shop say this to a customer's face?

Yuntera is

Yuntera is not

Hard-working. It talks about what it does: stores, delivers, pays.

Aspirational. No lifestyle, no “reimagining commerce”.

Plain. Says the price, the date, the fee and the reason.

Clever. No wordplay that makes someone read twice.

Respectful. Addresses every adult as usted.

Chummy. No forced Tico slang.

Costa Rican at the root. Señas, SINPE, colones, the GAM.

Costume. No flags, sloths or tourist imagery.

Honest about limits. “Todavía no entregamos en esa zona.”

A promiser. Never a date, a zone or a saving it cannot keep.

On the customer's side. Shows rights and fees before anyone asks.

Pushy. No countdowns, no pre-ticked boxes.

Calm. One exclamation mark in the whole product, for good news.

Loud. No capitals for emphasis, no emoji in the interface.

Voice and tone

Everything is written in usted, the everyday respectful register in Costa Rica. It is how people speak to customers and to strangers, so it suits a first-time shopper, a seller and a driver alike, and it never reads as cold.

Using the logo

Three colourings exist and no others: brand colour, ink and white. The rules for clear space, minimum size and misuse are set by this book; nothing in the code enforces them.

Brand on white, arena or any light tint. The default.

Clear space and minimum size

Keep a margin on every side equal to the diameter of one node, which is a quarter of the mark's height. One node should always fit between the logo and anything else.

Numbers and money

Money is where a marketplace earns or loses trust, so its typography is fixed, and all of it comes from one function. A price is never formatted by hand.

Precio final en colones. Incluye ₡2 387,40 de IVA.

  • ₡12 500The colón sign comes first, with no space after it.
  • 289 000Thousands are separated by a narrow no-break space (U+202F), never a dot or a comma. A price cannot wrap in the middle.
  • 2 387,40The decimal mark is a comma. Céntimos appear only when they exist.
  • −20%A real minus sign (U+2212) for discounts and deductions.
  • 0123456Tabular figures, so columns line up and a total does not jiggle when it changes.

Layout, spacing, radius, elevation

  • Width. Content sits in a column of at most 76 rem (1216 px), with a 16 px gutter that becomes 24 px from 768 px up.
  • Phone first. Every screen works at 390 px with no sideways scrolling. Wide tables scroll inside their own frame.
  • Spacing. There are no custom spacing tokens: the apps use the 4 px scale of the CSS framework, and space between siblings comes from gaps, not margins.

09

The wheel band

The brand has one decorative element: a quiet, repeating pattern of a dot inside a ring on a 40 px grid. It is an original geometric pattern. It nods to the country's tradition of painted oxcart wheels in general, a wheel seen from the side and repeated, without copying any real wheel's painted design.

Lo que ocupa, a su puerta.The shop's home-page headline is the one place it appears today.
  • Sparingly. One band per product is about right.
  • Only behind a large heading and a short lead line, with plenty of empty space. Never behind running text, forms, tables or prices.
  • Only in the two lightest celeste tints on celeste 50. It is not recoloured and not made bolder.
  • It is never replaced with photographs or reproductions of actual painted wheels.

Icons and illustration

The wheel glyph

The secondary glyph, for empty states and loading: a rim, a hub and eight spokes on the same 48 grid as the mark. As a spinner it turns once every 1.4 s, and stays still for people who ask their device for less motion.

Icons

The interface uses almost none. Words do the work: a button says Agregar al carrito rather than showing a cart. When an icon is added, it is drawn like the mark: square grid, one stroke weight, round caps and joins, one colour. No emoji as icons.

Product illustration

The demo catalogue has no photographs. Each product has a simple original drawing: an 800 × 800 square, a flat tint, one ink colour, 18-unit round strokes and a few solid shapes, with the object reduced to its most recognisable outline. Real listings carry the seller's own photographs.

12

Accessibility commitments

These are built into the tokens and shared components, so a screen gets them by using the system.

  • ContrastText meets WCAG 2.2 AA. The build fails if a token pair regresses.
  • FocusEvery interactive element shows the same 2 px ring from the keyboard. It is never removed.
  • TargetsControls are 44 px tall by default.
  • MotionPeople who ask their device for reduced motion get none.
  • Not colour aloneStatuses have words. Errors have sentences.
  • LabelsEvery field has a visible label, and errors are announced.
  • StructureOne h1 per page, real headings, tables with captions, a skip link.
  • LanguagePages declare es-CR. Sentences are short and every error says what to do next.
  • ZoomText can be enlarged. Body text is never under 16 px on a phone.
  • No dark patternsNothing is pre-selected at checkout except the shopper's own address. Consent boxes start unticked.
13

Photography and imagery

Sellers supply the photographs of their products. This is the guidance for their help pages and for anything Yuntera photographs itself. Photo upload is not built yet, so the demo uses the drawings above.

Product photographs

  • The whole product, in focus, filling most of a square frame.
  • A plain, light background. Daylight from a window beats a flash.
  • The real item the buyer will receive: real colour, real size. A hand or a common object for scale helps.
  • More than one angle, and a close-up of the label, the plug or the material.
  • No text, prices, phone numbers, borders or watermarks on the image.
  • No pictures taken from a manufacturer's or another shop's site without permission.

Yuntera's own imagery

  • Real places and people: a driver at a gate, a shop counter, a warehouse shelf. Ask permission first.
  • Never a customer's address, face or licence plate without consent.
  • Ordinary Costa Rica as it is. No flags, toucans, sloths or beaches standing in for “local”.
  • Natural light, and colours left as they are.
  • No stock photographs of smiling models holding boxes.
  • Never the wheel band or a colour wash over a photograph.
14

Legal notes and open items

Checked, not registered

  • On 21 November 2026 the domains yuntera.com, yuntera.cr and yuntera.app appeared free, and no app or business using the name was found. Method and limits are in docs/brand/naming.md.
  • Those checks are not a trademark search. Nothing should be printed, advertised or signed under the name until one is done.

Still for the owner to do

  • Register the domains. It is the only step that can be lost to someone else.
  • Trademark search and filing at the Registro Nacional for the name and the mark.
  • Reserve the social handles and the app-store names.
  • Create the support mailbox: the address shown in the apps is on a domain that is not registered yet.

Open items

Where the code and the brand do not yet agree, or a decision is still needed. The text source has the full list with detail.

  1. App accent colours are defined but not used. Each app is assigned a palette, yet every app is built in celeste and differs only by its descriptor. Either apply a restrained accent per app or delete the field. Guaria and maíz already mean promotion and rating, so using them as app accents would give them a second meaning.
  2. Small buttons: fixed. They were 36 px on every device. They are now 36 px with a mouse and 44 px wherever the pointer is a finger.
  3. Placeholder text: fixed. It was 3.73 : 1. It is now añil 500 (5.85 : 1) and the pair is in the contrast test.
  4. Three secondary taglines are defined and unused. They were reworded after the first review and now read Juntos llegamos más lejos, Usted vende. Nosotros lo llevamos. and Cada entrega cuenta. A native copy editor should still read them before any is used in public.
  5. Logo files for the outside world are incomplete. The lockups use live text. Outlined versions, white and ink lockups, PNG exports, app-store icons, a maskable icon and a sharing image do not exist yet.
  6. All apps share one favicon and one hard-coded theme colour. The theme colour is typed into each layout instead of being read from the tokens.
  7. There is no dark theme. Two dark-surface pairs are tested, but no app has a dark mode.
  8. English is declared but not built. Error messages have English text; the screens do not. An English voice has not been defined.
  9. Clear space, minimum sizes and misuse rules exist only in this book. Nothing checks them.
